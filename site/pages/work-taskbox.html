The project

Proof of work from very little input. Every screen, record and line of code on this page came from one person and a team of agents.

Ask a facilities firm to prove last Tuesday's fire door check happened and you'll often watch someone scroll through a WhatsApp thread. Taskbox turns the messages, photos and forms a crew already sends into proof their client can check. I set the direction and made the product calls. Agents specified it, built it, tested it and audited it.

The office types or says the work. The crew answers wherever they already are. The client gets the record.

The crew

Built for the phone in your pocket.

An iOS and Android shell around the same product, for crews who'd rather tap than type.

Sign in

A code, not a password. Crews get a six-digit code by email and type it straight into the app. Nothing to remember. Nothing to reset. Behind it, every session is hashed and checked on every request, sign-in attempts are rate limited, and switching someone off takes effect at once.

Today

What needs you, first. Late, blocked and flagged jobs, records waiting to be read and new messages all rise to the top of the day. Each has a single action. Lateness is worked out by one rule, never typed in by hand, so the list always matches what's happening on site.

Voice

Say it once. Taskbox does the rest. Type or speak a sentence, or paste a whole brief. Taskbox turns it into a card of changes. Confirm with one tap. Undo for five minutes. Fixed rules read the instruction first, and AI only fills in what the rules can't. Across a 300-message test bank, it has never made a wrong change.

Record

Submitted and frozen. Every finished job becomes a record with the answers, photos, times, signatures and who did the work. Once it's submitted it can't be edited. If something's wrong, it gets sent back with a reason, and that reason stays attached to the record for good.
