The project

A QME report decides most of what a workers’ comp case is worth, and it can run to hundreds of pages.

Defense attorneys carry 80 to 150 files each. Thin apportionment, impairments added instead of combined, a history that contradicts the deposition: if nobody raises it before the MSC, it usually stays. WilboLaw does the first read so the attorney’s time goes on strategy. It’s a review layer, not a drafting machine. Every issue it raises is tied to a page, and nothing leaves without a lawyer’s yes.

The file. Where the case is won or lost.

How it works

Read by the software. Decided by the lawyer.

WilboLaw does the reading, the checking and the first draft. The attorney makes the call.

01 · The file

Drop the whole file in.

QME and AME reports, supplementals, treating records, depositions and letters, scanned or not. Every page is read, typed and dated, and put on the case timeline.

02 · The issues

Know what’s wrong, and exactly where.

Each report is checked against the AMA Guides 5th edition, the Labor Code and the case law a defense attorney would cite: method, apportionment, Almaraz/Guzman, rating arithmetic and history. Each issue comes with its severity and the page it’s on.

03 · The letter

The fix, drafted from the problem.

An issue becomes a supplemental report request, a deposition outline or an objection, on the firm’s letterhead and in its format. Citations are checked before a draft can be approved. Anything left blank is marked.
