AI for its own sake achieves nothing.

Every business I meet has been sold a tool. Very few have been sold a story worth telling with it. Where the value actually sits, and how to find it before you buy anything.

Most businesses I meet have already bought something. A licence, a pilot, a chatbot on the website. Ask what it changed and the room goes quiet.

The tool is rarely the problem. It was bought before anyone wrote down the work it was meant to do.

Start with the work

Before I build anything, I ask who does the work today and where their hours go. Most of the answer is repetition: the same email, the same report, the same questions from customers.

That is where AI earns its keep. It takes the repetitive, high-volume work. Your people keep the judgement calls, with the data in front of them.

The technology goes in around your people, not over them.

Then the people

A tool nobody knows how to run is a cost line. So the skills go in with the build. Your team learns it while it is being made, and on handover day they own it.

Before you buy anything

  • Write down how the work gets done today, by the people who do it.
  • Mark what repeats.
  • Pick one part of the business and start there.

If that list turns out to be short, good. I would rather tell you no in week one than in month six.
