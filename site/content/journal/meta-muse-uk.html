Most AI tells you how to do the thing. Muse goes and does it.

It’s Meta’s AI agent, and it launched in the UK on 23 November. You give it a job. It goes off and does the clicking across websites and apps, then checks with you before anything important happens.

It’s free to start, for anyone 18 or over. You can use it in the Muse app, at muse.ai or on WhatsApp.

Here are five jobs worth handing over. Copy the prompt, change the details, see what comes back.

1. Christmas shopping without twenty tabs

Muse can check the same thing across a few shops and come back with a shortlist. Connect the shops you use, like Currys, Argos, M&S, ASOS or JD Sports. Or just let it browse.

Pick the one you want, check the total and approve the checkout yourself. One catch: Amazon currently blocks Muse, so it won’t shop there.

2. The inbox and the school diary

Connect Gmail or Outlook. Let Muse read and draft, but keep sending on approval only.

Then add: “When a new school date arrives by email, show me a calendar entry before you add it.”

Read every draft before it goes. The tone isn’t always right first time.

3. The holiday, the price and the delay

Give it your dates, your budget and your rules. Let it do the searching. easyJet, Jet2 and Trainline are all UK launch partners.

And if the flight home runs late: “My easyJet flight was delayed three hours. Find the booking in my email, check what compensation I’m owed under UK rules, and draft the claim for me to approve.”

4. Refunds and bills

The admin nobody wants. Muse can find the order, check the policy and write the chaser.

Same idea for bills: “Here’s my car insurance renewal. Find like-for-like quotes with the same cover and excess, and don’t buy anything until I’ve read them.” Do it again when your energy deal ends.

5. The saved Reels you never cook

Most of us have a pile of saved recipes we’ll never make. Connect Instagram and let Muse sort them.

Ocado and M&S work too. Same trick for a trip: “Turn my saved Reels about Lisbon into a three-day itinerary, with links to each place.”

Before you start

Three things. They take two minutes.

The new starter, again

It’s the same rule as every week. You don’t give a new starter the company card on day one.

Give it the inbox first. Check what it does. Then decide how much more to hand over.
