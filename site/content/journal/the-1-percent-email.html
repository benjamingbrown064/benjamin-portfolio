Most people I talk to about AI try to change everything at once. New system, new process, new way of working. Two weeks later it’s gathering dust and they’re back to doing it all by hand.

I think that’s the wrong way round. Get 1% better at one thing you already do every day. Then do it again next week.

For most of us, that’s email.

Here’s where this ends up.

Untrained:

Hi Sarah,

I hope this email finds you well! Thank you so much for reaching out regarding a potential call. I would be absolutely delighted to connect on Thursday at 2pm, as that time works seamlessly with my schedule. Please don’t hesitate to reach out if anything changes.

Best regards,
Benjamin

Trained:

Hi Sarah,

Thursday at 2pm works. I’ll send an invite.

Cheers,
Ben

Same request. One sounds like a machine. One sounds like you. The difference isn’t the AI. It’s the training.

One rule the whole way through: Claude drafts. You press send.

Day 1

1. Connect Gmail, and lock sending first

  1. In Claude, go to Customize > Connectors. Find Gmail, click Connect and sign in with Google.
  2. Open Gmail under Connectors, go to the Permissions tab, and set send_message, reply and forward to Always ask.

Now it can draft as much as it likes, and nothing goes out without your yes.

Using a company Google account and seeing "Access blocked"? Your Google admin needs to mark Claude as a trusted app. It takes about 15 minutes to kick in.

Not on Gmail?

  • Microsoft 365: go to Customize > Connectors > Microsoft 365. Work accounts usually need your IT team to switch it on once.
  • Outlook every day? Claude for Outlook is an add-in from Microsoft AppSource. It only ever drafts, never sends. It’s in beta, needs a paid Claude plan and a Microsoft 365 mailbox.
  • Anything else (iCloud, Yahoo): there’s no connector yet. Paste emails in and drafts out. Slower, same training.

2. Give it a home

In Claude, open Projects, create a new project and call it Email. Do everything below inside it.

3. Gather 40 of your emails

Claude learns from what you’ve actually sent. Ask it to find a good mix:

Search my Sent mail from the last 6 months. Find 40 emails I wrote myself: about 15 short replies, 15 normal emails, 5 longer ones, and at least 5 where I said no, chased something or gave bad news. Mix clients, my team and suppliers. Leave out forwards, auto-replies, templates and anything under 5 words. Show them as a numbered table. Don't analyse my style yet.

Scan the table. Take out anything confidential or odd.

4. Build your voice profile

Using only those emails, write my "Email voice profile". Describe only what you can see, quote real examples, and don't flatter me.

Cover: how I come across in three sentences, my typical length for a quick reply and a normal email, my exact greetings and sign-offs by type of person, how I ask for things, say no and give bad news, phrases I use often, things I never do, and how my tone changes between clients, team and suppliers.

Then add these hard rules, even where my emails break them:
- British English spelling. No em dashes.
- Match the length of the email I'm replying to. Most replies under 80 words.
- Never open with "I hope this email finds you well" or close with "Please don't hesitate to reach out", unless I really do.
- Never use: delve, leverage, seamless, robust, streamline, "I'd be happy to".
- No bullet points or bold unless I ask.
- The ask goes in the first two sentences.
- If you don't know a fact, write [CHECK: what]. Never make one up.
- Draft only. Never send, reply or forward unless I type "send it".

Finish with questions about anything you couldn't tell.

5. Check it like a new starter’s notes

Read it as if a new assistant had written it about you.

  • Is the sign-off exactly what you type?
  • Are the greetings right for each kind of person?
  • Delete anything that could describe anyone. "Professional yet approachable" tells it nothing.
  • Add your own pet hates. Answer its questions.

Then:

Here are my corrections and answers: [list]. Rewrite the full profile.

6. Save it

Open the Email project, edit its instructions and paste in the profile. Every chat in the project now starts with it. Keep a copy in a document called Email voice v1.

Want your voice outside the project too? Choose Use style > Create & edit styles > Create custom style > Add writing example, paste the profile and five of your best emails, and call it My email voice.

7. Run the test

Use the same five every time, so you can see the score move.

Calibration test. Using my Email voice profile, draft these 5 emails. Don't send anything.

1. Reply to [colleague] confirming Thursday at 2pm works for a call.
2. Chase [client] about an invoice that's 14 days overdue. Firm but friendly.
3. Turn down [supplier]'s proposal on price. Leave the door open.
4. Thank [team member] for [piece of work] and say what difference it made.
5. Tell [client] the project's two weeks late because [reason]. Offer a new date and one thing we'll do to make up for it.

Not running a business? Swap in your own five: a school reply, a party RSVP, chasing a refund.

A draft only passes if it gets all five:

CheckPass if
Would I send it?Yes, or one or two words changed
LengthAbout what I’d really write
Greeting and sign-offExactly mine
Banned words and em dashesNone
FactsUnknowns marked [CHECK], nothing made up

Expect about 3 out of 5. Don’t use it on real replies until you get 4.

When one fails, don’t fix the draft. Fix the rule:

Draft [number] failed because [reason]. Which rule in my profile was missing or unclear? Give me the exact wording to add.

Add it to the profile and run the test again.

Week 1

Use it on real replies

Inside the Email project:

Draft a reply to [name]'s latest email about [topic]. Don't send it.

Read it, change what’s wrong, send it yourself.

Talk instead of typing

This is where the time really comes back. On your phone, open the Email project, tap the microphone and just talk:

Turn what I'm about to say into an email in my voice. Keep only what matters. Draft it, don't send it.

Then ramble. "Tell Sarah thanks for the deck, it’s good, but slide four, the Q3 numbers look off, can she check before Friday, I’m presenting Monday, oh and well done on hiring Tom."

You should get four or five clean lines back, every point kept, nothing added. Do one a day.

Every Friday: your 1%

Ten minutes. Show Claude what you changed.

Weekly voice update. Here are this week's drafts and what I actually sent: [paste pairs]. What did I change more than once? Suggest no more than 3 new rules for my profile, worded exactly. Don't change anything until I say yes.

Approve the ones you agree with, save it as v2, rerun the test and write down the score. Watching that number climb is what builds the trust.

Week 4: let it watch your inbox

Only once you’ve scored 5 out of 5 twice in a row.

On a paid plan, click Scheduled in the left sidebar, then New task. Or describe the job in a chat and click Schedule when Claude suggests it. Choose Weekdays.

Every weekday morning, check my inbox for new mail since the last run. Ignore newsletters, receipts and anything I'm only cc'd on. For anything that needs me (a client, a deadline, money, a decision, a complaint, or someone waiting more than a day):
1. Add the label "Needs me".
2. Draft a reply in my Email voice profile, marking anything uncertain [CHECK].
3. Don't send, reply or forward anything.
Finish with a short list: who, what they need, by when.

Want it more than once a day? Set up a second task for the afternoon.

Because sending is set to Always ask, a scheduled check can’t send anything. It waits for you.

To send, open the draft in Gmail and press Send. Or say "send it" in Claude and approve the card that pops up.

Where I’d stop: don’t switch sending to Always allow. Claude reads, sorts and drafts. You press send.

Same rule, different tool

I run a small team of AI assistants. They read, they sort, they draft. None of them sends a thing without my yes. Autonomy on the boring bits. A person on the send button.

What it can’t do yet

  • See inside Gmail attachments. It sees the file names only.
  • Work on every company account without IT stepping in.
  • Check more often than once an hour.

You’ll need a paid plan for the Week 4 inbox check. Pro is $20 a month, or $17 a month billed yearly. UK readers see the pound price at checkout.

Your 1% this week

Don’t automate your whole inbox. Do Day 1. Write down your score.
