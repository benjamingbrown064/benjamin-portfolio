01The problem

The training existed. Nobody could find it.

5 October · WarrantyOS claims team

“The team are struggling to process claims in the app; I think it’s a training issue.”

They were right.

What they had

  • 25 written modules
  • Full of jargon
  • No videos
  • Filed under Settings, out of sight

What they needed

  • A video for every job
  • Plain English, on the real screens
  • Right where they work
  • Up to date, every time the app changes

Software training videos are the ones nobody films. The screens change every few weeks, so a video is out of date almost as soon as it’s finished. So nobody makes them, and new people learn by asking whoever is sitting nearest.

02The objective

Everyone knows how every screen works. Today.

Without anyone sitting them down.

01A video for every jobShort, narrated, filmed on the real screens.
02Where people need itOn the training page, next to the written steps.

Stuck on a live claim? The assistant on the claim page has read the same guides.

03The crew

One agent. A whole film crew.

A training video takes seven jobs. I wrote each one down as a skill, and gave them all to one agent.

01The writerReads the code and notes. Writes the guide with the exact button names.
02The scriptwriterOne sentence per click. Never names a button before it is on screen.
03The voiceOne British voice on every video. Energetic, not over the top.
04The camera operatorDrives the real app at the speed of the voice. Red box on what is being said.
05The editorDrops every sentence onto the frame its click starts. Adds the titles.
06The continuity checkerLooks at a frame from every sentence. Box in the right place? Click landed?
07The privacy guardSwaps every real name, email and number before it reaches the screen.

05The build

How one video gets made.

Eight steps. Seven are the agent’s. Tap one to jump to it.

1Write the guide2Script it3Voice it first4Film it5Sync it6Check it7Ship itThe only human step8Keep it current
1

Write the guide

The exact button names, in plain English.

The writer reads the code and the notes. Every guide ends the same way: what happens next, what to watch out for, and a question to check yourself.

2

Script it

One sentence per action.

The script never names a button before it is on screen.

3

Voice it first

The voice sets the pace.

Every sentence is recorded on its own, with its length saved. The camera waits for each one.

4

Film it

The real app, at the speed of the voice.

A red box lands on whatever is being said.

5

Sync it

A marker flicks. The sentence lands on it.

A tiny coloured square changes in the corner at every step. Each sentence is dropped exactly on its change, then the strip is cropped off.

6

Check it

A frame from every sentence. Checked by eye.

Is the box in the right place? Did the click land? Logs lie. Frames don’t.

7

Ship it

The only human step: approval.

Claude Code puts the video in the app and sends it to a person. Nothing goes live until they approve it.

The agent makes it. A person approves it.
8

Keep it current

One screen changes. One video is remade.

Every guide lists the buttons it uses, so only the videos that use a changed screen are remade, in a few minutes. Words unchanged? The voice is reused and costs nothing.

07Your 1% this week

You don’t need 164. Make one.

One video, this week.

About an hour. No agent needed.

  1. Pick the screen your team asks about most.
  2. Write the steps, one sentence per click, with the exact button names.
  3. Record yourself reading each sentence on its own.
  4. Record the screen while you click along at the pace of your voice.
  5. Put it on the page where people get stuck.

That’s the guide, the script, the voice and the film. The agent only does it faster, and keeps doing it.
