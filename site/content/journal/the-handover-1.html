If you use ChatGPT, Claude or Grok Bot and still feel a week behind, you are not alone. Most of what ships in AI is noise. This week was different. The tools a lot of us already pay for got closer to the work we already do, the best models got cheaper, and one report was a reminder that an AI account is also a bill.

Here are the five changes that matter to a business, and one thing to do with each.

1. AI can now sit in your meetings

On 11 November, Grok Bot 1.0 started joining Google Meet, Zoom and Microsoft Teams calls. It can answer in the meeting chat and send a summary afterwards. This is available on Team, Enterprise and Ultra plans.

The next day, Claude Meetings went into beta on Team and Enterprise plans. For now it works on Google Meet only. Claude joins as a visible participant, writes the notes and action items, and can start a document or a board while you talk.

Nobody has to type up the notes afterwards. The catch is the same as any first draft. It may miss the tone, mix up a name, or turn a loose idea into a firm action.

Try it this week on one internal meeting, not a client call. Read the action list before you share it, and correct anything that is not quite right.

2. Claude Fable 5.2 is faster and 20% cheaper

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 on 9 November. It is up to 30% faster than Fable 5.1, and 20% cheaper on the API, at $8 to send and $40 to receive per million tokens. It is built for ambitious, long, multi-step work rather than a single short answer.

If you already pay for a Claude subscription, you get the new model in the app. You do not need to think about tokens or API prices to use it.

If you have a long piece of work sitting unfinished, open it on Fable 5.2 this week. Give it a clear brief and an example of what good looks like.

3. You can now claim your business in ChatGPT

On 10 November, OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT. Any eligible business in a country where ChatGPT is available can claim its profile, keep its details up to date, and see how often ChatGPT mentions it and sends clicks its way. In the United States, some online shops and service businesses can also build a Business Agent, a branded assistant that customers can chat with.

This is the closest thing yet to a Google Business Profile for AI search. People already ask ChatGPT for a recommendation before they open Google. If your listing is empty or out of date, you are leaving that conversation to chance.

Go and claim your business this week. Check what topics already cite you, and fix any details that are wrong or missing.

4. You do not need the most expensive model for every job

Google spent the week cutting prices. On 10 November it released Gemini 4 Carbon, its strongest model, at an introductory $2 to send and $10 to receive per million tokens until 30 November, then $4 and $20. On 12 November it followed with Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a faster, cheaper option at $1.50 and $7.50.

Tokens are simply the units these services use to measure how much text you send and receive. The price is what you pay for every million of those units.

If you only ever reach for the most expensive model, you will spend more than you need to. Use the stronger model for hard, long work, and a cheaper, faster one for drafting, sorting and first passes.

Pick one routine task you already run through ChatGPT, Claude or Grok Bot. Try it on a cheaper or faster model this week, and keep the stronger one for the work that actually needs it.

5. A stolen AI key ran up a $740,000 bill

On 10 November, Railway published a report on an October security attack. Attackers went after customers’ AI keys, and unauthorised use of those stolen keys ran up an estimated $740,000.

You do not need to be a Railway customer for this to matter. An AI key is a password that can spend money in your name. If it leaks, the bill can move faster than anyone notices.

Treat every AI key like a company card this week. Set a spending limit where the provider allows it, and rotate anything that has been sitting around for months.

Also worth knowing

Grok Bot on Mac can now work with Messages, Mail, Notes, Reminders, Calendar, Location and Contacts. Claude launched Sheets, Whiteboards and Data on all paid plans, so plans and simple spreadsheets can stay in Claude. ElevenLabs Scribe v3 makes 27% fewer mistakes on recorded audio, and 43% fewer on live calls, than the previous versions. Anthropic, the company behind Claude, started the roadshow for its stock market listing, with shares expected to start trading on 19 November under the ticker ANT.

One to watch, though it did not ship this week: OpenClaw Enterprise, launched on 29 September, is a free tool a company can run on its own servers to keep AI assistants under its own security and permissions. It is still pre-1.0 and only suitable for pilots, but it is worth watching.

The takeaway

The tools got closer to where you already work, and the best ones got cheaper. None of that means you can switch them on and walk away. A meeting summary still needs a human check. A cheaper model still needs a clear brief.

Give the tool a job, look at what comes back, and only then let it go further. That is how the businesses getting real value from this are working, and it will still be true next week.
