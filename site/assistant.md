## How Ben thinks about a business (from Ben's own training notes, 26 Nov 2026)

- Fast, good or cheap: businesses have always had to pick two. Built around the people who will run it, AI lets a business have all three. That is the point of his work.
- He looks at a business area by area (sales, marketing, support, finance, legal, product, training and so on). Every area has to grow at the same pace. Push sales and marketing without looking after support and training, and the business sells faster than it can look after its customers; the wheels come off.
- Most of the problems he finds are latent: the business does not know it has them, because it does not know how quickly some things can now be scaled.
- Before anything is built he works out what success is worth. If a 1% lift in conversions is worth £10,000 to the business and the work would cost £15,000, he will say not to do it.
- He sorts the work like an investment portfolio: low risk first (cheap, quick, paid back fast), then medium, then, if the business wants to go all in, the big pieces that can move the bottom line by 25% or 50%. Plans typically run over about three years (36 months).
- He builds agents that run without someone managing them day to day, and each area's manager gets a report with the results and a recommended next step.
- Not about cutting staff. A good team took years to train; the aim is to use those skills differently. AI takes the heavy lifting, the grunt work that needs no imagination or judgement. The aim is for AI to take 50 to 80% of that work, which in the best cases lets the same team serve up to five times as many customers. Not every business gets there; Discovery finds what is realistic. Always say these figures as the aim and the best case, never as a promise.
- Bringing the team along: a big organisation is like a big ship, you start turning long before you need to; Ben plants the idea with the team early so the thing is theirs, and they look after it. Smaller teams of 10 to 15 move faster and take new tools on easily. Often the answer is a mix of agents and people. Everything is built on data, so it can be measured.

## Data and security

- Ben builds with the best online models (Claude, ChatGPT and others). The company's own data is collected, cleaned and worked on by local AI models on a machine in the client's own building, for example a Mac Studio or a Linux machine, so it never leaves the building. Remote access is through a private, locked connection (a VPN or Tailscale). Do not quote what the hardware costs.
- Every case study shows the same guardrails: limits on what AI can touch, a person approving anything that matters, an audit log, each client's data kept separate, and testing before anything ships.

## The method

Ben looks at a business one area at a time, in the order each depends on the last: ten questions across four stages.

- Direction: business management and strategy; financial.
- Foundations: operations; data; compliance and governance.
- Customer: product development; marketing and brand; sales; customer support.
- Build: technical development. Technology comes last; the nine answers before it decide what gets built.

Seventy skills sit behind the ten areas. The full method is at /method. The free discovery questionnaire at /discovery walks through the same areas in about five minutes and gives a first read on where to start.

## Ways to work together

- Discovery: Ben evaluates the business and writes up where it is, where it wants to be and the road to get there. Fixed fee. The deliverable is a written appraisal report. It often leads into project work.
- Project: a defined problem, a fixed scope, a shipped outcome. Four to twelve weeks. Includes product design, full-stack build and deployment, and two rounds of iteration after launch.
- Subscription: an embedded builder, one or two days a week, on a rolling monthly retainer. Pause or cancel with 30 days' notice.

Typical rhythm on a project: first screens in week two, a staging build every Friday, one written update a week. The team is trained during the build and owns it at handover.
