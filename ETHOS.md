# Ethos

This site is not a hire-me page. It plants a flag.

## The flag

We are getting lost in the glamor of using AI, as if using it is the win. It isn’t. The why is the win. The how is the win. The result is the win.

People want credit for saying they did the thing. Benjamin is only focused on the result. Hour or 25 hours — it doesn’t matter. The skill is whether the work fits the objective, and how it lands for the person on the other side.

AI is 80/20. The 20% is curation, decision, opinion, the paint on the canvas. A business run on more than that is soulless. Use AI to make the existing team more efficient, more profitable, and to deliver a better working tomorrow. Not to replace the point of having them. Not to get a clap for using the tool.

## The golf analogy

No one tees off without looking at the flag.

That is the governing idea. Use it with restraint. It is not a sports theme: no clip-art golfers, no grass, no country club. The flag is the objective. Look at it before you swing.

## His words

Keep this spoken voice. Light grammar and line-break edits only. Do not punch it up.

I think we live in a moment where people want credit for saying they did the thing. They want the kudos. They want to show they used AI. I’m not interested in that. I’m interested in the result. Did the thing actually do what it was supposed to do? If that takes an hour, great. If it takes 25 hours, great. I don’t care. I care about the end product.

Most people will sit down, get AI to write an email, and send it. They don’t stop and think about the objective. They don’t think about how that email is going to feel to the person who receives it, or whether it even belongs in that world. That’s where I come in. That’s the skill. Building the thing that actually delivers the result, not building something so I can say I built something.

AI is an 80/20 rule. Eighty percent of the work can be done with it now. It’s the twenty percent that makes it what it is. The curation. The decision. The opinion. The paint on the canvas. That’s the person. You cannot run a business on more than that. If you do, it’s a soulless business, and I’m not interested in that.

What I want to sit down and ask is pretty simple. How do we become more efficient? How do we generate more profit? How do we deliver a better working tomorrow? You’ve already got a team. We use AI to benefit them, to improve them, and to get a better day out of the one they’re already in. Not to replace the point of having them. Not to get a clap for using the tool.
